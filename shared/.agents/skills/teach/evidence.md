# Evidence

Why the invariants hold. Read when tempted to skip one or when asked.

| Rule | Evidence | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Answers during practice harm later performance; hints instead of answers remove most of the harm | Bastani et al., PNAS 2025, ~1,000 students: plain GPT-4 +48% in practice, −17% on the exam vs no access; hint-only tutor +127% in practice, exam equal to control | Large field RCT; guardrails prevented harm, did not clearly add learning |
| Concrete procedural rules beat "be a tutor" | Kestin et al., Sci Reports 2025 (AI tutor beat active-learning class); LearnLM/Eedi RCT 2025 | RCTs; LearnLM messages were human-reviewed |
| Models leak the answer by default, at every size | Log analysis, 10,512 queries: >96% first-response leakage | Observational |
| Questions beyond readiness hurt retention | ZPD/scaffolding reviews; GuideEval shows LLMs fail to adapt to confusion | Reviews + benchmark |
| Learning from syntheses is shallower than synthesizing yourself | Melumad & Yun, PNAS Nexus 2025, n=10,462; links did not fix it | Large; outcomes self-report and advice quality |
| Offloading regulation reduces transfer | Fan et al. 2025: essays improved, transfer did not; 2026 reviews on metacognitive laziness | One study + reviews |
| Self-explanation with feedback aids transfer | Aleven & Koedinger; 2026 calculus replication with LLM feedback | Strong older ITS work; small new study |
| Worked examples for novices, fade for experts | Expertise-reversal literature | Strong |
| Retrieval, spacing, calibration feedback | Memory research | Strong |

Not established: any of this on conceptual subjects (all trials are math, physics, code, writing); retention beyond weeks; whether the combination beats its parts. The ledger is the test.
